# Historical Sites on Pangkor Island (Dutch & British Periods)

List of historical places and sites on Pangkor Island, Perak, Malaysia associated with the Dutch East India Company (VOC) era (17th–18th century) and the British Straits Settlements administration (1874–1935), ordered chronologically by built or establishment period.

## Chronological List

1. **Kota Belanda (Dutch Fort / Fort Dingdingh)** (1670; rebuilt 1743; restored 1973)
   - 17th-century fortified brick godown/warehouse built by the Dutch to store and monopolize Perak tin. Reconstructed in 1973 on its original foundations at Kampung Teluk Gedung.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/559410700) (4.2005°N, 100.5759°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Kota+Belanda,+Kampung+Teluk+Gedung,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

2. **Site of the 1693 Dutch Stone Pillar** (1693)
   - Erected following the 1690 massacre of the Dutch garrison by Malays under Panglima Kulup as a marker of Dutch sovereignty bearing the VOC and United Provinces coats of arms. It repeatedly toppled, deteriorated, and no longer stands; its symbolic function was replaced in 1743 by the permanent engravings on Batu Bersurat.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap (Teluk Gedung vicinity)](https://www.openstreetmap.org/?mlat=4.2000&mlon=100.5758#map=18/4.2000/100.5758) (4.2000°N, 100.5758°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Kampung+Teluk+Gedung,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

3. **Batu Bersurat Belanda (Tiger Rock / Sacred Rock)** (1743)
   - Large granite boulder roughly 140 meters south of Kota Belanda, carved by Dutch soldiers with the VOC emblem, the coat of arms of the United Provinces (Dutch lion), the year 1743, and soldiers' initials. Sheltered by a protective pavilion.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/559410703) (4.1993°N, 100.5761°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Batu+Bersurat+Belanda,+Kampung+Teluk+Gedung,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

4. **HMS Pluto Treaty Anchorage (Waters off Pangkor)** (20 January 1874)
   - Anchor site in the Dindings Strait where Straits Settlements Governor Sir Andrew Clarke convened Perak Malay chiefs and signed the historic Pangkor Treaty aboard the colonial steamer *HMS Pluto*.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap (Dindings Strait)](https://www.openstreetmap.org/?mlat=4.2150&mlon=100.5850#map=14/4.2150/100.5850) (4.2150°N, 100.5850°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Selat+Dinding,+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

5. **Pangkor Town Government Jetty Site (Terminal Jeti Pangkor)** (1880s–1930s)
   - Original colonial public landing jetty connecting Pangkor Island with the Dindings colonial administrative headquarters at Lumut across the strait; modernized today into the main passenger ferry terminal.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/560380349) (4.2134°N, 100.5762°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Terminal+Jeti+Pangkor,+Pekan+Pangkor,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

6. **Pangkor Town Main Street (Jalan Besar)** (Late 19th – early 20th c.)
   - Primary commercial thoroughfare and coastal road surveyed and surfaced during the Straits Settlements period, linking the jetty to settlements at Sungai Pinang.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/?mlat=4.2145&mlon=100.5750#map=18/4.2145/100.5750) (4.2145°N, 100.5750°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Jalan+Besar,+Pekan+Pangkor,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

7. **Bukit Pangkor Survey Baseline / Trig Station** (Late 19th – early 20th c.)
   - Highest point of the island (~350 m), accessible only by foot trail via Pasir Bogak. Site of colonial trigonometric survey benchmark stones used for maritime cartography of the Malacca Strait.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/7932911812) (4.2391°N, 100.5620°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Bukit+Pangkor,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

8. **South Pangkor Forest Reserve & Ranger Outposts (Hutan Simpan Pangkor Selatan)** (1920s)
   - Gazetted under Straits Settlements Forest Ordinance (Ordinance 108) with boundary markers and ranger boatman patrols to curb unauthorized timber extraction; continues today as a permanent state forest reserve.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/?mlat=4.2050&mlon=100.5650#map=15/4.2050/100.5650) (4.2050°N, 100.5650°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Hutan+Simpan+Pangkor+Selatan,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

9. **Cross-Island Road (Jalan Pasir Bogak)** (1920s–1930s)
   - Trans-island road cut by British Public Works through the island's lowest natural saddle, linking eastern Pangkor Village to western Pasir Bogak beach (approx. 2 km). In the colonial era, this road terminated at Pasir Bogak; the continuation northward along the west coast toward Teluk Nipah and Teluk Dalam was a late-20th-century development (Jalan Pasir Bogak, State Route A207). Built by the Dindings Rural Board / PWD to access the breezy western shore for colonial health convalescence and sea-bathing (sanatorium/rest house), improve police patrols against maritime smuggling and piracy on open waters, and provide overland transit without hazardous sea journeys around the southern cape during monsoon swells.
   - Location: [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/41411779) (4.2140°N, 100.5650°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Jalan+Pasir+Bogak,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)

10. **Pangkor Village Public Latrine & Sanitation Site** (1927)
    - Public latrine, culverts, and sanitary drainage built by the Dindings Rural Board / PWD to combat malaria and cholera. Located behind the central public market and bazaar near the landing shore; now the modern MPM municipal public toilet and bazaar civic area.
    - Location: [OpenStreetMap (Bazaar Pangkor / Pekan Pangkor)](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/560380368) (4.2131°N, 100.5756°E) | [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps?q=Bazaar+Pangkor,+Pekan+Pangkor,+32300+Pulau+Pangkor,+Perak)
